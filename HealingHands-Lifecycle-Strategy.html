Healing Hands Lifecycle Strategy

We began with a customer journey mapping exercise to gain a high-level view of Healing Hands' existing lifecycle program, from how the brand shows up in search, social, and AI, through purchase and post-purchase. This audit identifies the highest-impact opportunities to grow lifecycle revenue by fixing the flows that already carry the program, converting an audience that opens but doesn't click, and building the second order into a repeatable habit.

01 · The Story

The flows already carry the program. They're just leaking at every seam.

Healing Hands has the structure most brands are still trying to build: automation drives the majority of revenue on a fraction of the sends. The problem isn't that the machine is missing, it's that every stage of it underperforms its own benchmark — Welcome at half, Abandoned Cart at a fifth, and two in three customers who never come back for a second order. Our roadmap organizes around three strategic themes, each tied to an outcome.

Theme 01
Cuts across both

Fix the flows that already do the work

Flows earn 68% of program revenue from 11% of sends, but Welcome sits at $1.32 RPR against a ~$2.35 benchmark and Abandoned Cart at $0.69 against $3.00–3.65 — the single biggest gap in the program.

Folding the winning Abandoned Checkout structure into Cart, reworking Welcome 2 and 3, and adding sunset logic fixes the highest-leverage surface area in the program without building anything net new.

The layer that amplifies Themes 02 and 03
Theme 02
Conversion

Earn the click, not just the open

Email opens run 58% — well above norm — while campaign clicks sit at 0.64% against a 2–5% target. Campaigns carry 89% of send volume for 32% of revenue at $0.04 per send.

Clearer offers and product-led modules, a calendar rebalanced toward what actually earns, and paid creative threaded into lifecycle so what someone clicked shapes what they see next.

Target: campaign click rate 0.64% → 2%+
Theme 03
Retention

Win the second order

Two in three customers are one-and-done, so median LTV of ~$131 is largely unrealized. The reorder clock is predictable at ~59 days and nothing in the program is timed to it.

A replenishment flow on the 59-day clock, a real post-purchase cross-sell, and a loyalty program that makes the second order feel like membership rather than another discount.

Target: second-order rate up, at zero acquisition cost

03 · The Second-Order Gap

The second order is where lifetime value is made, at zero acquisition cost.

Scrubs are consumable and the reorder timing is predictable. Nothing in the program is currently built around either fact.

Headline finding

Two in three customers never place a second order.

Median LTV sits at ~$131 and is largely unrealized. The reorder clock runs at ~59 days, which is short enough to build a flow around and predictable enough to time store credit to. The customers are already acquired and already paid for — the second order costs nothing to win.

›
Five-star reviews are an untapped trigger.

A customer who just told us they love the product is the highest-intent replenishment and bulk-buy audience in the file. Today that signal goes nowhere.

06 · Competitive Analysis

FIGS wins on identity. Cherokee wins on efficiency.

The two most useful comparison points sit on either side of Healing Hands — one proves the ceiling, one proves the mechanics.

FIGS

Closest competitor

  • Customers are worth more: $216 revenue per active customer, $114 AOV, NPS 80 (trailing 12 months).
  • No loyalty or points program. They run on community and identity — referral, birthday reward, newsletter coupons.
  • Retention comes from brand, not rewards: an ambassador program of diverse healthcare specialties, embedded cause work (student grants, nurse mental health), and seasonal or limited-edition color drops.
  • They grow LTV by leaning into wardrobe expansion — underscrubs, outerwear, layers.

Cherokee

Careismatic sibling — workhorse / value products

  • Positioned around "built for everyone" with wider distribution (uniform retailers, nursing schools) and accessible pricing.
  • Word of mouth is the acquisition driver.
  • More efficient dollars per send: ~$1/send on flows, with SMS Welcome strong at ~$4/send.
›
The read: identity can outperform discount, and the sibling brand proves the flow economics.

FIGS holds a higher-value customer with no points program at all, which is the case for building loyalty around belonging rather than a percentage. Cherokee's flow efficiency is the near-term proof that Healing Hands' flow benchmarks are reachable inside the same org, on the same platform.

07 · What Ties It Together

The same five threads run through all of it.

These aren't five separate problems. They're the reasons the same issues keep surfacing across different flows.

Everything answers price

Cart, browse, and welcome all lead with a discount. Fit, returns, guarantees, and color guidance are the objections that actually go unanswered.

Triggers ignore the clock we already have

The ~59-day reorder window and sets-purchased are both known and neither drives a trigger today.

Winners aren't being promoted

Tests are running and producing results — the text hero variant, the checkout structure — but the wins aren't shipping as defaults.

Identity is underused

Nurses Week is a top earner and FIGS runs its whole retention story on identity. Occupation isn't captured anywhere yet.

List hygiene is missing

No sunset logic means deliverability risk on the open rate that's currently the program's biggest strength, plus avoidable overage.

09 · Open Questions & Data Follow-Ups

Three things to resolve before or during the readout.

Raised in the working doc and carried forward here so they get answered rather than buried.

1 · Does the sunset and list-cleaning work extend to Cherokee?

Sunset logic is a Healing Hands quick win, but the deliverability and overage case is brand-agnostic. If Cherokee has the same gap, this should be scoped once and shipped across both rather than twice.

Confirm with: Dang / Mark

2 · Will Browse Abandonment volume drop once the underlying issue is resolved?

The recommendation to tighten targeting to repeat and high-dwell browsers assumes today's trigger volume is real. If the issue raised in chat is inflating entries, the sizing on this changes and the targeting fix may be smaller than it looks.

Blocked on: issue resolution before sizing

3 · Is Welcome actually suppressing Abandoned Cart, or is the attribution clean?

The working hypothesis was that Welcome suppresses Cart revenue. The attribution model is linear (email: 1-day open, 3-day click; SMS: 1-day click), so credit should be split evenly and suppression shouldn't be the explanation. This needs one more pass before we conclude the Cart gap is real and not an artifact — and it should be settled before we judge any Cart rebuild against baseline.

  • Expand the higher-converting SMS leg, which is the fastest lift here since it's copy only.

Big Swings

Four structural bets behind the retention and conversion gaps.

Higher lift and higher ceiling than the quick wins. Each cuts across more than one flow, and the last two need buy-in beyond Lifecycle.

1 · The full Post Purchase cross-sell flow

Two in three Healing Hands customers never come back, and median LTV of ~$131 sits mostly unrealized as a result. The second order is where lifetime value is made and it costs nothing in acquisition to win. The bet is to build the flow around two things the data already tells us: what someone bought, and when they'll need more.

Time it to the clock we already have
  • Build a replenishment flow timed to the ~59-day reorder window, triggered off sets purchased plus elapsed time rather than a fixed calendar date.
  • Someone who bought one set is a different conversation than someone who bought four. Quantity should change both the timing and the message.
Complete the set, then expand the wardrobe
  • Recommend coordinated items against what was actually purchased — set completion first, since it's the easiest yes.
  • Follow FIGS into wardrobe expansion: underscrubs, outerwear, layers. It's how they grow LTV and it's a category Healing Hands can speak to.
Use the signals sitting idle
  • Trigger replenishment and bulk-buy prompts off 5-star reviews. Someone who just said they love it is the highest-intent reorder audience in the file.
  • Roll out the winning "text hero" variant as the default here rather than continuing to split traffic against a known loser.
What it needs: a product-level view of what constitutes a "set" so coordinated recommendations are accurate, and confirmation that review events are flowing cleanly into Klaviyo as triggerable signals.

2 · Loyalty program relaunch

Reorder timing is predictable at ~59 days, scrubs are consumable, and Nurses Week performance suggests identity outperforms discount for this audience. FIGS holds a materially more valuable customer ($216 per active customer, NPS 80) with no points program at all — which says the moat is belonging, not a percentage. Loyalty should be the front door to the program, not a tab on the site.

Launch mechanics
  • Launch on earn and redeem — store credit back per dollar spent. Simple enough to explain in a subject line.
  • Make loyalty the main offer entry: "sign up for 15% off" routes into the program rather than dropping a flat code.
  • Capture occupation and specialty in the signup flow. That single field unlocks identity and tenure segmentation for everything downstream.
Embed it, don't isolate it
  • Post-purchase states credit earned and progress to the next reward.
  • Cart and checkout can reference a store-credit balance instead of a fresh discount.
  • Winback can lead with "you have $X waiting" rather than training customers to wait for a code.
Future phase
  • Time store credit expiry or bonus windows to the 59-day reorder clock, so the incentive lands exactly when the need does.
  • Borrow from what works next door: FIGS runs ambassadors across diverse specialties and embeds cause work. Occupation capture is what makes that addressable.
What it needs: pending relaunch with Rise.ai and gated on the Shopify migration. Cross-functional with product and site teams because it changes signup capability, not just email. Tiering (flat vs. tiered, milestone or career-anniversary tiers) isn't defined — recommend a scoped loyalty PRD kickoff before build.

3 · Winback on RFM cohort movement

Today's lapse logic runs on a clock. The better trigger is movement between RFM cohorts — catching someone the moment their behavior changes rather than waiting for a fixed number of days to elapse on a customer who may have already gone.

Trigger on the change, not the calendar
  • Enter the flow when a customer drops a cohort, with exit logic the moment they purchase.
  • Layer in the inputs we already have: how many sets they previously bought and where they sit against the reorder window.
Escalate message before discount
  • First touch reminds and resurfaces the color or collection they bought. Incentive comes second, deadline third.
  • A lapsed one-time buyer and a former repeat buyer are different conversations and shouldn't get the same email.
What it needs: RFM scoring stood up in Klaviyo and agreement on cohort definitions before the flow can trigger on movement between them.

4 · Integrate paid creative into lifecycle

When someone enters lifecycle, all we keep is an email address — even though they already told us a great deal by which ad they clicked. With opens at 58% and clicks at 0.64%, the creative and offer are the gap, and the most relevant creative we have is the one that already earned their attention.

Thread the signal through
  • Carry the color and style someone clicked in paid into the popup, welcome series, and abandonment emails.
  • Someone who clicked a specific collection should see that collection in the emails that follow, not a generic bestsellers grid.
Fix how we test while we're here
  • Run fewer, bigger 50/50 A/B tests with a pre-set primary metric and an order threshold agreed before launch.
  • The current pattern produces results that never quite conclude. Fewer tests with real power beats more tests with none.
What it needs: cross-functional buy-in from paid media and product, plus an agreed ad taxonomy and UTM structure so creative attributes scale beyond a first manual list.
