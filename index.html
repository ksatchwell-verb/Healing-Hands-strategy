Healing Hands Lifecycle Strategy

We began with a customer journey mapping exercise to gain a high-level view of Healing Hands' existing lifecycle program, from how the brand shows up in search, social, and AI, through purchase and post-purchase. This audit identifies the highest-impact opportunities to grow lifecycle revenue: optimizing the flows already in place, increasing conversion across leads, and building loyalty and cross-promotion across the portfolio.

01 · The Story

The foundation is strong. The opportunity is acquisition.

Healing Hands earned $521.7K from January to May 2026 on an automation-led program that is genuinely working: flow sends earn ~$0.68 each against $0.04 for campaigns, Welcome email one beats its benchmark, and Nurses Week produced a record month. Our roadmap organizes around three themes, each tied to an outcome.

Theme 01 · Crawl
Foundation

Fix and strengthen the foundational flows

As we dig into the data, we keep finding foundational fixes underneath a program that looks healthy on the surface. The Abandoned Cart flow, for one, was set up on the wrong trigger, so it never reached part of the people it should have. We have already fixed that one, and we expect to find more like it as the audit continues.

Work through the foundational flows first and get the triggers, timing and audiences right before layering optimization on top. Solid plumbing is what makes the gains in Themes 02 and 03 real instead of cosmetic.

The base the rest of the roadmap builds on
Theme 02 · Walk
Acquisition

Increase conversion rate across leads

Lead-to-first-order conversion runs at ~20% against Cherokee's ~30% in July, and has been flat while Cherokee has climbed. This is where Healing Hands has the most headroom, and it is the mirror of Cherokee, whose opportunity sits in retention.

Welcome emails 2 and 3 are the lever. Email one converts at 2.68%, ahead of the ~2.3% benchmark, so the pattern that works is already in the flow and can be extended.

Target: close the gap on Cherokee's ~30%
Theme 03 · Run
Loyalty

Loyalty and cross-promotion

Repeat rate of ~33% is ahead of Cherokee's ~25%, so retention is a strength to build on. The bigger prize is portfolio-wide: today cross-promotion is a creative tactic rather than a lifecycle mechanic.

A phased loyalty program, earn and perks first, cross-brand introduction later, turns two brands serving the same profession into one relationship.

Target: loyalty live, then cross-brand in phase 3
The numbers behind it

Healing Hands brings in about half the revenue Cherokee does ($521.7K vs $1.07M), but it holds onto customers better. Around 1 in 3 buyers reorder (33% vs Cherokee's 25%) and median lifetime value is higher ($131 vs $97). Acquisition is the mirror image: Cherokee turns about 30% of leads into a first order, versus about 20% for Healing Hands. Retention is the strength here. Acquisition is the gap to close.

02 · Competitive Analysis

The gap to FIGS is frequency, not basket size.

First-order AOV is ~$118 against FIGS' $124, so this is not a pricing problem. Cherokee is the same company selling to the same nurses at a lower price point, and the two brands have opposite strengths: Cherokee converts leads better, Healing Hands keeps customers better.

FIGS

Closest competitor

  • Customers are worth more than ours. $220 revenue per active customer (+6% YoY), $124 AOV (+4%), 3M active customers (+12%), net revenue up 28%. Q1 2026 figures.
  • No loyalty or points program. They seem to run on community and identity: referral, birthday reward, newsletter welcome, student and military.
  • Retention comes from brand, not rewards. An ambassador program of diverse healthcare specialties, cause work (student grants, nurse mental health), and seasonal or limited-edition color drops.
  • They seem to grow LTV through "wardrobe expansion": underscrubs, outerwear and layers. Build the full kit.

Cherokee

Careismatic sibling, workhorse / value products

  • Positioned around "built for everyone", with wider distribution (uniform retailers, nursing schools) and accessible pricing.
  • Word of mouth is the acquisition driver.
  • More efficient dollars per send. $0.88 per send on flows, and SMS Welcome is strong at ~$4 per send.
  • Converts leads at ~30% against our ~20%, on the same platform with the same team. Their opportunity is retention; ours is acquisition.

Quick Wins

Five moves that build on what already works.

Acquisition first, then retention. None require a net-new build.

1

Rebuild Welcome emails 2 & 3

Goal: extend email one's success across the rest of the series.
  • Email one converts at 2.68%, ahead of the ~2.3% benchmark, so the pattern that works is already in the flow.
  • Emails 2 and 3 open at 39-40% but convert at ~0.15%, so the audience is engaged and waiting for a reason to act.
  • Lead email 2 with the fit and returns guarantee, the objection a discount does not answer.
  • Add a color and collection guide, and introduce "build your rotation" early.
Metric: lead conversionTest: welcome messaging
2

Optimize Abandoned Cart

Goal: bring the main cart flow up to the $1.27 Abandoned Checkout already achieves.
  • Healing Hands leads this test, with Cherokee taking post-purchase, so the two brands cover more ground between them.
  • Start by confirming the trigger population and the Welcome attribution split, so we have a clean baseline to measure the rebuild against.
  • Audit timing. First touch should land within 30 to 60 minutes.
  • Rebuild message 1 on Checkout's blueprint, which converts at ~2%.
  • Scale the SMS leg, at $1.45 RPR against email's $0.69, and promote the leading variants ("AbandonedCart_1 (Repeat)" earns $1.80).
Metric: Cart RPROwner: Healing Hands test
3

Launch the post-purchase flow

Goal: set up the second order at no acquisition cost.
  • Filed as quick win one on Cherokee at Dang's request, and Cherokee leads the testing here with the winners carrying over.
  • Post Purchase already opens at 44% on buyers, so the attention is there.
  • When someone buys a piece belonging to a set, recommend the coordinating item.
Metric: post-purchase RPROwner: Cherokee test
4

Use what Cherokee has already proven

Goal: stop paying to learn the same thing twice.
  • Launch Cherokee's Abandon Cart SMS copy on Healing Hands directly.
  • Standing principle: whichever brand runs a test first, the other inherits the winner.
  • Four tests have been running since 2025 without a clear enough result to call. Rebalance them to 50/50 and set an order threshold so they can be settled.
  • Two are close to conclusive already: the Post Purchase text hero and Browse Variation B are both leading, and both need a little more runtime.

Revamp the winback series and its timing

Goal: reach a lapsed audience the current flow is not converting.
  • The existing six-email series earns $0.07 RPR, so there is meaningful headroom in a revamp rather than a new build.
  • Test against dynamic Klaviyo segments (at risk, needs attention, inactive) instead of the static audience.
  • Move the reorder window from four months to two, closer to the ~59-day median between orders, so the trigger lands while intent is live.
  • Escalate message before discount, and talk to a lapsed one-time buyer differently than a former repeat buyer.

Big Swings

Three structural bets that cut across the whole journey.

Higher lift and higher ceiling than the quick wins. Each touches more than one area of the journey and needs buy-in beyond Lifecycle, which is the test for what belongs here.

1 · Cross-sell across the journey

Healing Hands sells a basket comparable to FIGS and sells it ~1.7 times. Frequency is the opportunity and cross-sell is the mechanic that addresses it. This is separate from the loyalty build and can be tested in email now, without waiting on the platform work.

Where it can be tested, in email today
  • Post Purchase. Set completion against what was actually bought.
  • Abandoned Cart. Coordinating items alongside the recovery message.
  • Welcome 2 and 3. A collection and color guide is a soft cross-sell.
  • Browse Abandonment. Adjacent category rather than the item browsed.
  • Winback. Wardrobe expansion as the reason to return, instead of a discount.
What the tests would answer
  • Whether set completion beats category expansion as the opening move.
  • Whether the FIGS wardrobe-expansion play works on our catalogue.
  • Whether cross-sell beats discount as a reason to buy again.
What it needs: a product-level view of what constitutes a set, so recommendations are accurate rather than generic. That is the single dependency across every surface and is worth scoping once rather than per flow.

2 · Loyalty program relaunch

Healing Hands sells a basket comparable to FIGS at ~$118 first-order AOV against their $124. It just sells it once, averaging ~1.7 orders per customer against FIGS' $220 per active customer per year. The entire gap is frequency, which makes it a lifecycle problem rather than a brand or product one. FIGS closes it with no points program at all, running on identity and belonging, and Nurses Week performance says that works on this audience too. Loyalty should be the front door to the program, not a tab on the site.

What the retention data already shows
  • Healing Hands repeat rate is healthy for apparel and ahead of Cherokee. This is not a retention rescue, it is compounding an advantage that already exists.
  • Nurses Week is the proof point: 31 campaigns drove $45.8K, 28% of all campaign revenue, and SMS hit the best RPR of any theme. Identity already outperforms discount here for one week a year.
  • The upside is the second order specifically: mature cohorts add 30% to 37% per buyer after the first order, front-loaded into the first two months, against a ~59-day clock the program does not currently act on.
Phase 1: earn and perks
  • Launch on earn and redeem: store credit back per dollar spent. Simple enough to explain in a subject line.
  • Make loyalty the main offer entry: "sign up for 15% off" routes into the program rather than dropping a flat code.
  • Capture occupation and specialty in the signup flow. That single field unlocks identity and tenure segmentation for everything downstream.
Phase 2: embed it across the journey
  • Post-purchase states credit earned and progress to the next reward.
  • Cart and checkout can reference a store-credit balance instead of a fresh discount.
  • Winback can lead with "you have $X waiting" rather than training customers to wait for a code.
Phase 3: cross-brand
  • Introduce the sibling brand inside the program once earn and redeem is established and understood.
  • Shared or interoperable points would make cross-brand movement a lifecycle mechanic rather than a creative tactic. That is a portfolio decision, not a Healing Hands one.
What it needs: pending relaunch with Rise.ai and gated on the Shopify migration. Cross-functional with product and site teams because it changes signup capability, not just email. Tiering is not defined, so recommend a scoped loyalty PRD kickoff before build.

3 · Personalization by persona

When someone enters lifecycle, all we keep is an email address, even though they already told us a great deal by which ad they clicked. With opens at 58% and clicks at 0.64%, the creative and offer are the gap. This runs across all brands rather than one: whatever is learned on Cherokee carries directly to Healing Hands, and the opportunity is personalization through ads rather than creative threading alone.

Classify what is in the ad
  • Phi's MVP classifies ad creative into personas and themes (explorer vs. deal-seeker, plus pain point, desired outcome and emotional driver) and outputs Klaviyo-ready fields. Same initiative as Cherokee.
  • UTMs alone only give lead source. The value is understanding what is actually in the ad.
Thread it into lifecycle
  • Carry the color and style someone clicked in paid into the popup, welcome series, and abandonment emails.
  • Route by persona: deal-seekers see offers, explorers see discovery.
Testing changes that go with it
  • Run fewer, bigger 50/50 A/B tests with a pre-set primary metric and an order threshold agreed before launch.
  • The current pattern produces results that never quite conclude. Fewer tests with real power beats more tests with none.
Start here: break month-0 lead conversion out by source. Cohorts flatten fast enough that a source can be read inside 30 days, and it is the fastest way to tell whether the 2026 softening is lead-mix or lifecycle. What it needs: cross-functional buy-in from paid media and product, plus an agreed ad taxonomy and UTM structure with New Engen so persona fields scale beyond a first manual list.
