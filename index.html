Healing Hands Lifecycle Strategy

We began with a customer journey mapping exercise to gain a high-level view of Healing Hands' existing lifecycle program, from how the brand shows up in search, social, and AI, through purchase and post-purchase. This audit identifies the highest-impact opportunities to grow lifecycle revenue: optimizing the flows already in place, increasing conversion across leads, and building loyalty and cross-promotion across the portfolio.

01 · The Story

The foundation is strong. The opportunity is acquisition.

Our roadmap for Healing Hands organizes around three themes, each tied to an outcome.

Theme 01 · Crawl
Foundation

Fix and strengthen the foundational flows

As we dig into the data, we may find foundational fixes underneath a program that looks healthy on the surface. The Abandoned Cart flow, for one, was set up on the wrong trigger, so it never reached part of the people it should have. We can set up a global holdout to better understand the impact of lifecycle on revenue.

Work through the foundational flows first and get the triggers, timing and audiences right before layering optimization on top. A strong foundation is what can amplify the gains in Themes 02 and 03.

The base the rest of the roadmap builds on
Theme 02 · Walk
Acquisition

Increase conversion rate across leads

Lead-to-first-order conversion runs at ~20% against Cherokee's ~30% in July, and has been flat while Cherokee has climbed. This is where Healing Hands has the most headroom, and it is the mirror of Cherokee, whose opportunity sits in retention.

The welcome series is the lever. Email one converts at 2.68%, ahead of the ~2.3% benchmark, so the pattern that works is already in the flow and can be extended across the series.

Target: close the gap on Cherokee's ~30%
Theme 03 · Run
Loyalty

Loyalty and cross-promotion

Repeat rate of ~33% is ahead of Cherokee's ~25%, so retention is a strength to build on. The bigger prize is portfolio-wide: today cross-promotion is a creative tactic rather than a lifecycle mechanic.

A phased loyalty program, earn and perks first, cross-brand introduction later, turns two brands serving the same profession into one relationship.

Target: loyalty live, then cross-brand in phase 3
The numbers behind it

Healing Hands brings in about half the revenue Cherokee does ($521.7K vs $1.07M), but it holds onto customers better. Around 1 in 3 buyers reorder (33% vs Cherokee's 25%) and median lifetime value is higher ($131 vs $97). Acquisition is the mirror image: Cherokee turns about 30% of leads into a first order, versus about 20% for Healing Hands. Retention is the strength here. Acquisition is the gap to close.

02 · Competitive Analysis

The gap to FIGS is frequency, not basket size.

First-order AOV is ~$118 against FIGS' $124, so this is not a pricing problem. Cherokee is the same company selling to the same nurses at a lower price point, and the two brands have opposite strengths: Cherokee converts leads better, Healing Hands keeps customers better.

FIGS

Closest competitor

  • Customers are worth more than ours. $220 revenue per active customer (+6% YoY), $124 AOV (+4%), 3M active customers (+12%), net revenue up 28%. Q1 2026 figures.
  • No loyalty or points program. They seem to run on community and identity: referral, birthday reward, newsletter welcome, student and military.
  • Retention comes from brand, not rewards. An ambassador program of diverse healthcare specialties, cause work (student grants, nurse mental health), and seasonal or limited-edition color drops.
  • They seem to grow LTV through "wardrobe expansion": underscrubs, outerwear and layers. Build the full kit.

Cherokee

Careismatic sibling, workhorse / value products

  • Positioned around "built for everyone", with wider distribution (uniform retailers, nursing schools) and accessible pricing.
  • Word of mouth is the acquisition driver.
  • More efficient dollars per send. $0.88 per send on flows, and SMS Welcome is strong at ~$4 per send.
  • Converts leads at ~30% against our ~20%, on the same platform with the same team. Their opportunity is retention; ours is acquisition.

Quick Wins

Four moves that build on what already works.

Acquisition first, then retention. Read this as continuous optimization across phases, not a couple of one-off tests: each item is a program that compounds, and most extend flows that already work rather than net-new builds.

1

Rebuild the welcome series through behavior-based personalization

Goal: turn a two-email tweak into a personalized welcome program that closes the lead-conversion gap.
  • Split the series into paths by who someone is and how they behave, using Klaviyo signals: on-site search (a search for "cotton" or "tops" is a clear content cue), plus what they browsed and abandoned.
  • Build the content around that behavior, FIGS style: build your rotation, break down fabrics, contrast tops and bottoms, lead with fit and the returns guarantee.
Metric: lead conversionTest: behavior-based welcome paths
2

Optimize and expand the abandoned flows

Goal: extend beyond abandoned cart to the full set of abandonment flows, adding touches rather than only tuning existing ones.
  • Expand the SMS leg and add touches across the flows, since SMS already outperforms email here ($1.45 RPR against $0.69).
  • Test messaging written for a younger audience, in step with the persona work.
  • Personalize off ad creative and other Klaviyo signals, using the data in the trigger payload: what someone browsed, abandoned, or searched.
Metric: abandoned-flow RPR
3

Launch the post-purchase flow

Goal: set up the second order at no acquisition cost.
  • Build a multi-touch sequence, in the range of five touchpoints, in place of the single trigger that exists today.
  • When someone buys a piece from a set, recommend the coordinating item and build toward the full rotation, FIGS style.
  • Tailor content to what they bought, from fit and fabric care to the pieces that round out the outfit.
  • Time it to the reorder window, front-loaded into the first two months when the second order usually lands, against the ~59-day median between orders.
Metric: post-purchase RPR
4

Revamp the winback series and its timing

Goal: reach a lapsed audience the current flow is not converting.
  • Trigger off dynamic Klaviyo segments (at risk, needs attention, inactive) rather than the static list, so each person enters as their behavior starts to slip.
  • Move the first touch from four months to two, closer to the ~59-day median between orders, so it lands while intent is still live.
  • Segment the message: a lapsed one-time buyer gets a reason to give the brand a second try, a former repeat buyer gets what is new since they last shopped plus their past favorites.
  • Escalate value before discounting: lead with new arrivals, best-sellers, and restocks in their size or category, and hold the code for the final touch.
  • Personalize off what they browsed, bought, or searched, so the series shows the fabrics, fits, and categories they already signaled.
Metric: reactivation rateTest: 2mo vs 4mo window
Big Swings

Three structural bets that cut across the whole journey.

Higher lift and higher ceiling than the quick wins. Each touches more than one area of the journey and needs buy-in beyond Lifecycle, which is the test for what belongs here.

1 · Personalization by persona

Healing Hands and Cherokee sell to different people, so the same lifecycle plays should not look the same. Healing Hands skews younger and more fashion-forward, closer to a Fabletics-style lifestyle brand where community is about style and identity. Cherokee is the practical, reliable, grassroots choice where community is about the profession.

Read the persona split
  • Healing Hands: modern, style-led, lifestyle and community. Messaging leans into who you are and the community you join.
  • Cherokee: product features, durability and value. Messaging leans into the everyday scrub you can buy five of and rely on.
  • Same tactics across both brands, different execution and creative.
Lean on New Engen's learnings
  • New Engen is rapidly testing 300+ ad creatives across both brands and filtering by spend to find what works. Pull the winning creative and messaging into lifecycle instead of guessing.
  • Set up a working session with them to align on what is landing, plus an ad taxonomy and UTM structure so the signal scales into Klaviyo.
What we would test for Healing Hands
  • UGC and influencer content woven through the flows, not treated as a one-off creative tactic.
  • Copy written for a younger, style-led audience, against Cherokee's feature-and-durability angle.
  • Channel mix, including SMS-first where the audience skews younger.
  • Thread the color and style someone clicked in paid into the popup, welcome, and abandonment emails, and route deal-seekers to offers and explorers to discovery.
How we test it
  • Rapid testing is the method. Fewer, bigger 50/50 tests with a pre-set primary metric and an order threshold, so results actually conclude off a thin baseline.
  • Whatever is learned on one brand carries to the other, adjusted for persona.
What it needs: alignment with New Engen on creative, ad taxonomy and UTMs, plus buy-in from paid media and product. Run the UGC and lifestyle creative direction by the client team before it scales.

2 · Cross-sell across the journey

Frequency is the opportunity and cross-sell is the mechanic that addresses it. This is separate from the loyalty build and can be tested in email now, without waiting on the platform work.

Where it can be tested, in email today
  • Abandoned Cart. Coordinating items alongside the recovery message.
  • Welcome 2 and 3. A collection and color guide is a soft cross-sell.
  • Browse Abandonment. Adjacent category rather than the item browsed.
  • Winback. Wardrobe expansion as the reason to return, instead of a discount.
What the tests would answer
  • Whether set completion beats category expansion as the opening move.
  • Whether the FIGS wardrobe-expansion play works on our catalogue.
  • Whether cross-sell beats discount as a reason to buy again.
What it needs: a product-level view of what constitutes a set, so recommendations are accurate rather than generic. That is the single dependency across every surface and is worth scoping once rather than per flow.

3 · Loyalty program relaunch

Healing Hands already keeps customers better than it converts them, so the real prize is frequency: the second and third order, not just the first. A loyalty program is the mechanic that drives that broader engagement, a reason to come back that is not another discount, and it should be the front door to the program, not a tab on the site. Because the two brands mean different things to their customers, the same program should be messaged differently: for Healing Hands, loyalty is about belonging to a modern, style-led community, with access, newness and status as the rewards that matter; for Cherokee, it leans on everyday value and reliability for the working nurse. Same mechanic, different story.

What the retention data already shows
  • Healing Hands repeat rate is healthy for apparel and ahead of Cherokee. This is not a retention rescue, it is compounding an advantage that already exists.
  • The upside is the second order specifically: mature cohorts add 30% to 37% per buyer after the first order, front-loaded into the first two months, against a ~59-day clock the program does not currently act on.
Phase 1: earn and perks
  • Launch on earn and redeem: store credit back per dollar spent. Simple enough to explain in a subject line.
  • Make loyalty the main offer entry: "sign up for 15% off" routes into the program rather than dropping a flat code.
  • Capture occupation and specialty in the signup flow. That single field unlocks identity and tenure segmentation for everything downstream.
Phase 2: embed it across the journey
  • Post-purchase states credit earned and progress to the next reward.
  • Cart and checkout can reference a store-credit balance instead of a fresh discount.
  • Winback can lead with "you have $X waiting" rather than training customers to wait for a code.
Phase 3: cross-brand
  • Introduce the sibling brand inside the program once earn and redeem is established and understood.
  • Shared or interoperable points would make cross-brand movement a lifecycle mechanic rather than a creative tactic. That is a portfolio decision, not a Healing Hands one.
What it needs: pending relaunch with Rise.ai and gated on the Shopify migration. Cross-functional with product and site teams because it changes signup capability, not just email. Tiering is not defined, so recommend a scoped loyalty PRD kickoff before build.
